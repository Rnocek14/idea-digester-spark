# Historical imagery for the Shore Path stops

Where to get "then and now" photographs of Geneva Lake, what each archive
actually permits, and the traps that would land a correction — or worse — on a
local news site.

Research caveat: this was assembled from search-index content because the
research environment could not open the archives' pages directly. Rights language
below is a reliable guide to **where each institution stands**, not a first-hand
quotation. Re-read the live terms before publishing anything, especially the
Wisconsin Historical Society's and the University of Chicago's.

## The rule that governs everything

**As of 2026, US works published in 1930 or earlier are in the public domain.**
Ninety-five year term, expiring 1 January of the 96th year — so 1930 works became
free on 1 January 2026, and 1931 works arrive on 1 January 2027.

"Published" is doing real work in that sentence. See trap 3.

## Where to go, in order

### 1. Library of Congress — start here
Best licensing anywhere, and more Geneva Lake material than expected. LOC states
plainly that it **does not license, charge permission fees, or grant or deny
permission to publish**. Directly downloadable to high-res TIFF, no login, no fee.
Append `&fo=json` to any search or item URL for structured metadata including
rights fields.

Four useful veins:
- **FSA/OWI black-and-white negatives** — Arthur Rothstein shot Lake Geneva in
  July 1942: lakefront, excursion boats, sailing, vacationists. Federal work
  product, explicitly *"in the public domain and free to use and reuse."* The
  cleanest material available.
- **Detroit Publishing Company** — c. 1898–1905 Geneva Lake views.
- **Panoramic Photographs** — includes a 1910 view *from the Yerkes dome*.
- **HABS** — Geneva Hotel, survey WI-283. Federal, public domain.

Identified items worth pulling first:

The 1942 Rothstein frames are sequential (…5761, …5769, …5787), so there are

collection for `Lake Geneva` directly.

### 2. UW–Madison Digital Collections — "Images of Lake Geneva"
https://search.library.wisc.edu/digital/ALakeGenImg

**The most on-topic body of Geneva Lake imagery online**: 500+ souvenir
photographs and postcards from the Lake Geneva Public Library's local history
collection, plus plat books and atlases 1873–1962. IIIF for content, OAI-PMH for
metadata, so it can be indexed programmatically.

Rights are **unclear and item-by-item**: UW says it generally does not own the
rights and points users to item-level information and to Lake Geneva Public
Library. UW is neither granting a licence nor asserting one — for pre-1931
postcards you are relying on your own public-domain determination, which is fine.

Best use: **as a research index.** Find what exists here, then clear rights
yourself or source an independent copy.

### 3. Geneva Lake Museum — the partnership to build
255 Mill St · (262) 248-6060 · staff@genevalakemuseum.org

Thousands of historical photographs: early settlers, the estates, steamboat
regattas — and specifically **1932 Riviera construction and interior images**,
which nobody else appears to hold. No online catalogue, no published rights
policy, nothing downloadable. Everything by appointment and negotiation.

This is the highest-value relationship for a recurring then-and-now series, and
museums in this position routinely trade scans for credit and promotion. Email
them with a specific proposal.

### 4. Wikimedia Commons
[Lake Geneva, Wisconsin](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Lake_Geneva,_Wisconsin)
(101 files) and [Geneva Lake](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Geneva_Lake)
(40 files). Every file carries an explicit licence tag — but verify each, because
Commons has bad tags. Mostly modern with some historical.

### 5. Wisconsin Historical Society — only for the must-haves
The most restrictive terms of the majors: **permission is required for any use
beyond display in a private residence**, no published fee schedule (you submit a
form and receive a quote), and WHS holds rights to some items while others carry
donor restrictions.

Worth paying for anyway, because these are genuinely unique:

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| IM117881 | Lake Geneva waterfront from a boat — **Whiting House Hotel and a sidewheel steamboat**. Ideal then-and-now anchor. |
| IM112615 | Black Point, exterior of the Seipp summer home |
| W01463T | Steamer *Loreley* approaching the Black Point pier |
| W01464J | Aerial of Black Point Estate |
| W014FYG | Beach, Fontana |

Note WHS **operates Black Point Estate** and holds the Seipp family photographs,
so the best Black Point imagery sits behind the tightest terms. Order by WHI
number. Permission form:
https://www.wisconsinhistory.org/pdfs/PDF-Permission-Request-Form.pdf

### 6. University of Chicago Photographic Archive — for Yerkes
The real source for historic Yerkes **building and construction** photography.
Educational use permitted with a mandatory credit line; **commercial projects
require permission and may carry a fee — and an ad-supported news site is
commercial.** https://photoarchive.lib.uchicago.edu/rights.html

### 7. Chicago History Museum — good value, situational
**Flat $50 per digital file** for images with no known copyright restrictions,
after which reuse is free. Relevant because Geneva Lake was Chicago money's
summer colony — Seipp, Wrigley, Wacker. $50 flat with clear reuse beats an opaque
quote. https://images.chicagohistory.org/terms-of-image-use/

### 8. Newberry Library — permissive, relevance unconfirmed
Open access: *"use images from the Newberry collection for any lawful purpose
without paying any licensing or permission fees."* No Geneva Lake photographs
confirmed; its strength here would be maps, plat books and Chicago family papers.

### 9. Yerkes glass plates — skip for this purpose
YFF owns ~180,000 astronomical glass plates (1900–1970), but these are
**photographs of the sky, not of the observatory or the lake**, and most are not
digitised. Contact YFF as the site operator, not as an image source.

## The traps

**1. "No known restrictions" is not a public-domain finding.** It means the
archive found no restriction it could identify. LOC says outright that material
coming from the Library *"is not an indication that it is public domain."* The
1910 Yerkes panorama even names a copyright claimant — it is safe because 1910 is
before 1931, not because of the advisory. Do the date arithmetic every time.

**2. The archive's terms bind you even when its copyright claim doesn't.** Under
*Bridgeman Art Library v. Corel*, 25 F. Supp. 2d 421 (S.D.N.Y. 1998), a faithful
photographic reproduction of a flat public-domain work gets no new US copyright —
so WHS cannot hold copyright in a scan of an 1890s photograph. **But its
terms-of-use are a contract**, and ordering the file accepts them regardless of
the underlying work's status. Two consequences:
- Obtain a file *from* an archive → honour that archive's terms. It is cheap and
  it preserves the relationship.
- Obtain a pre-1931 image from **an independent copy you own** → no archive's
  terms reach you.

That second path is the strongest tactic available: **buy original pre-1931
Geneva Lake postcards and scan them yourself.** There is a live market
([CardCow](https://www.cardcow.com/viewall/66379/), eBay). You own the object,
the image is public domain by publication date, no institutional terms apply, and
the scan is usually better than what an archive will sell. For shoreline, pier
and streetscape then-and-now pairings, postcards are exactly the right source.

**3. Published and unpublished run on different clocks.** The 1930 cutoff is for
*published* works. An unpublished photograph — a family snapshot, a studio
portrait never distributed — runs life-plus-70, or **120 years from creation** for
anonymous or unknown authors. So an unpublished 1910 estate photograph is not free
until 2030. Local museum holdings are full of exactly this. Always ask the
archivist whether an image was ever published, and where.

**4. There is no public-domain route to a Riviera photograph before 2028.**
Construction began 2 March 1932; formal opening 22 May 1933. Every photograph of
the building is at most 94 years old: 1932 images enter the public domain on
1 January 2028, 1933 images on 1 January 2029. Either license from the Geneva
Lake Museum or wait. A decorative then-and-now pairing is a weak fair-use case —
don't rely on it.

**5. "Lake Geneva" means Switzerland to most catalogues.** LOC and Commons both
carry substantial Lac Léman material under these names; Commons'
`Category:Lake Geneva` and `Category:Paddle steamers of Lake Geneva` are Swiss.
Search `"Lake Geneva, Wis"` or add `Walworth`. Note also that the water is
**Geneva Lake** and the city is **Lake Geneva** — querying both surfaces
different results.

**6. You are a commercial user.** Nearly every "free for educational and
non-profit use" clause above — UW, UChicago, WHS's fair-use carve-out — does not
cover an ad-supported news site. Do not read those permissions as applying here.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Harvest LOC now.** No permission needed. The 1942 Rothstein set, the Kayes
   Park views, the 1910 Yerkes panorama, the 1882 bird's-eye, HABS Geneva Hotel.
   Enough for a first run.
2. **Index UW's "Images of Lake Geneva"** via IIIF/OAI-PMH, filter to items
   published 1930 or earlier, self-clear. Highest relevance per unit of effort.
3. **Buy and scan pre-1931 postcards** for locations LOC and UW don't cover.
   Sidesteps every permission problem.
4. **Email the Geneva Lake Museum** proposing a credited ongoing partnership —
   they hold the Riviera and steamboat material nobody else has.
5. **WHS only for specific must-haves** — the Whiting House sidewheeler and the
   Black Point images are genuinely unique. Budget for a quote.
6. **UChicago Special Collections** for historic Yerkes construction photography;
   expect a commercial fee and the mandatory credit line.

## How this connects to the schema

`shore_path_stops` carries, per stop:

- `historical_image_url`, `historical_image_year`
- `historical_image_credit` — the credit line the archive requires
- `historical_image_source_url` — the item page, so a reader can check
- `historical_image_rights` — one of `public_domain`, `no_known_restrictions`,
  `licensed`, `permission_granted`

**Nothing renders without both a credit and a rights value.** `public_domain` and
`no_known_restrictions` are deliberately separate values because they are
different claims — see trap 1. Whoever loads an image records what the archive
actually permits, not what age implies.
